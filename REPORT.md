## Dataset

The data is a public dataset, available for download from IBM's own public GitHub repository (also on Kaggle). There are 7,043 rows and 21 columns for each row, one of the fictional telecom service customers. There are nine service columns, demographics (gender, senior citizen, partner, dependents), account details (tenure in months, contract type, billing/payment method, monthly charges, total charges). The goal is Churn - customer churned in the past month.

I chose it because it's a business decision we can make easily (who offers them a retention offer) and because actually feature engineering does matter. There are most categorical columns that require encoding. There are several service columns that are the same (the top one is the same as six of them: “No internet service”). The TotalCharges is a text field that contains 11 blanks. The size will fit nicely into the repo. The only real warning is that the data is synthetic data that was generated by IBM, not actual customer records.

## Business / real-life framing

An at-risk call is made to a retention team that offers a €50 discount. The model prioritizes customers according to their probability of churning, indicating who to call. The 27% churn rate is deceptive; if it is 73%, that's me, a naive prediction, and that is churn. No date column, so no future information will leak when stratified random split. If the offer retains 30% of churners, each selling for €780 (12 months of revenue), a churner lost due to the offer is worth €184 while a wasted offer is worth €50. Therefore, the threshold should be well below the 0.5 level, but in theory it should be around 0.21 and be determined by minimizing the total cost.

## Data preparation & feature engineering

I then tried to change the columns 'TotalCharges' to number and replaced all 11 blank cells with 0 (all 11 were new customers, and thus had a tenure of 0). Engineered features: number of services subscribed (proxy for engagement); tenure groups, because the churn risk is high at the beginning, but drops sharply after one year.
